# X (Twitter) writing & growth principles

Background reference for drafting on X. This is Ledi's own condensed synthesis of ideas from Nicholas Chua's ["the science behind going viral on X"](https://x.com/nicholaschua/status/2035072826508812749) guide — reorganized and rewritten, not a copy. Read it, don't quote it back at length; pull in a specific point when a drafting decision calls for it.

## 1. Build taste before writing anything

Scroll the feed on purpose, not passively. When a post stops your scroll, ask why: what's the hook, what's the dominant reaction (likes vs. bookmarks vs. replies), what format is it using. Keep a running collection of posts that worked, sorted by format, so when you're stuck on how to phrase something you have real reference points instead of starting cold — first-person vs. third, terse vs. anecdotal, lowercase vs. proper casing.

## 2. Decide what the account is for

A new or growing account generally earns trust faster by leading with its own story and credibility (what the founder/CTO/company has actually built or learned) rather than acting as a curator/aggregator of other people's content. Curating others' work can build reach fast, but it's a second phase, not a starting strategy — earn the audience on your own material first.

## 3. Match the format to the message

- **Short statements** (plain takes/opinions): easy to write, but they mostly ride on the author's existing authority — a big account can post a one-liner and have it land; a smaller or newer account usually can't. Use sparingly for this reason.
- **Long-form single posts**: a compressed narrative — context, what happened, the takeaway. Good for a specific story that's more than a one-liner but doesn't need a multi-step breakdown.
- **Threads**: for content with genuine sequential structure (a how-to, a chronological build-up). These get bookmarked heavily because readers want to come back to a step-by-step later.
- **Long articles**: best for broad, widely-relevant topics where there's real depth to share. The length itself is the point — asking for a real reading-time commitment breaks people out of pure scroll behavior, but only works if the substance backs it up.

## 4. How the post reads in-feed matters as much as the content

Both a regular post (truncated after the first stretch of characters) and an article (previewed by its first few lines) are judged by their opening before anyone commits to more. The opening needs to promise something concrete enough that clicking "Show more" feels worth it — and that click itself is a signal to the algorithm that the post is worth showing to more people, not just a UX detail.

## 5. Consistency of quality beats posting frequency

"Just post every day" undersells what actually matters: deliberate, value-driven posts outperform volume. An established, high-following account can carry low-effort posts on reputation alone; a smaller account generally can't, and copying that pattern without the underlying authority tends not to work. Better to run fewer, more intentional experiments — pulling from real experience, projects, or resources — than to post filler on a schedule.

## 6. Not all engagement counts equally

Likes, bookmarks, and replies aren't weighted the same by the algorithm — replies/conversation carry far more weight than likes. Two practical implications: (1) pick which kind of reaction a given post is realistically aiming for (an article aims for bookmarks, an announcement aims for comments, a relatable post aims for likes) and shape it accordingly; (2) actually reply to comments after posting — engagement begets distribution, and going quiet after posting signals the opposite.

## 7. Quote-tweeting can revive a post

When someone quotes a post, the impressions on that quote count toward the original post's total reach. That means quote-tweeting your own older post with a fresh angle or one-liner can meaningfully extend its life — useful for a post that started slow, not just for cross-promotion.

## 8. None of this matters without actually posting

Analysis and planning only go so far — the accounts that grow are the ones that ship consistently and tolerate the posts that don't land. Don't let this checklist become a reason to overthink and under-post.
